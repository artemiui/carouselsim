# Technical Architecture & Simulation Dynamics of EDSA CarouselSim

## 1. System Overview

EDSA CarouselSim is a discrete-event microscopic transit simulation modeled after the 24-station Epifanio de los Santos Avenue (EDSA) Busway Bus Rapid Transit (BRT) corridor in Metro Manila, Philippines. The engine simulates multi-berth median station contention, bidirectional passenger flows, vehicle kinematics, rogue dwelling behavior, and corridor-level shockwave propagation.

The simulation runtime is implemented in Python using `SimPy` for process-oriented discrete-event modeling and `NumPy` for stochastic distributions. The web client renders dynamic telemetry via dual synchronized topological representations: a calibrated geometric route schematic and a dual-ring concentric circulation carousel.

---

## 2. Demand Modeling & Passenger Generation

Passenger arrivals at each station $i \in \{1, \dots, 24\}$ follow an inhomogeneous Poisson process:

$$N_i(t, t + \Delta t) \sim \text{Poisson}\left(\lambda_i(t) \cdot \Delta t\right)$$

where the time-varying arrival rate $\lambda_i(t)$ is defined as:

$$\lambda_i(t) = \lambda_{i, \text{base}} \cdot M_i(t)$$

- $\lambda_{i, \text{base}}$: Baseline passenger arrival rate per minute calibrated to station capacity and historical passenger volumes.
- $M_i(t)$: Dynamic rush-hour multiplier corresponding to simulated time of day $t$.

### 2.1 Directional Origin-Destination (OD) Matrix

Upon arrival, each passenger is assigned a destination station $j \neq i$ via a time-dependent discrete probability distribution:

$$P(D = j \mid O = i, t) = \frac{W_{ij}(t)}{\sum_{k \neq i} W_{ik}(t)}$$

The attraction weights $W_{ij}(t)$ model asymmetric commuter flow patterns in Metro Manila:
- **Morning Peak ($07:00 \le t < 09:30$):** Inflow predominantly southbound toward major Central Business Districts (Ortigas, Buendia, One Ayala) with directional bias factor $\gamma_{\text{SB}} \approx 3.5 \times 2.5$.
- **Evening Peak ($17:00 \le t < 20:00$):** Outflow predominantly northbound toward residential terminal hubs (Fernando Poe Jr., Balintawak, Monumento) with directional bias factor $\gamma_{\text{NB}} \approx 3.5 \times 2.5$.
- **Midday Peak ($11:30 \le t < 13:30$):** Commercial centroid attraction (SM North EDSA, Trinoma) with multiplier $\gamma_{\text{mid}} \approx 4.0$.

Each passenger is queued in the dedicated directional platform buffer: $\mathcal{Q}_{i, \text{forward}}$ (Southbound) or $\mathcal{Q}_{i, \text{reverse}}$ (Northbound).

## 3. Bus Motion, Kinematics, and Network Geometry

The transit corridor spans $23.8\,\text{km}$ along EDSA. Inter-station link travel times are computed from OpenStreetMap highway segments:

$$T_{\text{travel}}(i \to i+1) = \frac{d(i, i+1)}{v_{\text{lane}}} + \delta_{\text{U-turn}}$$

- $d(i, i+1)$: Geodesic path distance between station nodes along the dedicated median lane.
- $v_{\text{lane}}$: Mean operating speed within the busway, regulated by speed limits ($35\text{--}50\,\text{km/h}$).
- $\delta_{\text{U-turn}}$: Stochastic operational delay modeling Bagong Barrio surface turning conflicts:
  $$\delta_{\text{U-turn}} \sim \begin{cases} U(45\,\text{s}, 150\,\text{s}) & \text{with probability } p_{\text{u-turn}} = 0.25 \\ 0 & \text{otherwise} \end{cases}$$

## 4. Multi-Berth Queueing & Platform Dwell Dynamics

### 4.1 Berth Resource Contention

Each station platform $i$ features $c_{i}$ dedicated physical berths per direction, modeled as a finite multi-server queuing resource $M/G/c_{i}$. When bus $b$ arrives at station $i$, it must acquire an unoccupied berth:

$$\text{Delay}_{\text{berth}}(b, i) = t_{\text{docking}} - t_{\text{arrival}}$$

If all $c_i$ berths are occupied, incoming buses form an upstream physical queue:

$$L_{\text{queue}} = N_{\text{queued}} \cdot L_{\text{bus}}$$

where $L_{\text{bus}} = 12.0\,\text{meters}$. Upstream backups exceeding $150\,\text{meters}$ produce bottleneck shockwaves that block intermediate lane progression.

### 4.2 Dwell Time Formulation

Dwell times follow the Transit Capacity and Quality of Service Manual (TCQSM) standard, incorporating passenger exchange volumes, door actuation overhead, and interior crowd friction:

$$T_{\text{dwell}} = \begin{cases} 
0 & \text{if } N_{\text{on}} = 0 \land N_{\text{off}} = 0 \land \neg \text{Terminal} \\
T_{\text{overhead}} + \max\left(N_{\text{on}} \cdot t_{\text{board}}, N_{\text{off}} \cdot t_{\text{alight}}\right) & \text{otherwise}
\end{cases}$$

Where:
- $T_{\text{overhead}} = 4.0\,\text{s}$ (door opening, driver clearance, closing).
- $N_{\text{off}}$: Passengers whose destination matches station $i$.
- $N_{\text{on}} = \min\left(\text{Capacity} - \text{Occupancy}, |\mathcal{Q}_{i, \text{dir}}|\right)$.
- Interior crowd friction factor:
  $$t_{\text{board}} = t_{\text{board}, 0} \cdot \left(1 + 0.45 \cdot \left(\frac{\text{Occupancy}}{\text{Capacity}}\right)^2\right) \cdot \xi_{\text{board}}$$
  with $t_{\text{board}, 0} = 2.5\,\text{s/pax}$ and $\xi_{\text{board}} \sim U(0.92, 1.15)$.
- $t_{\text{alight}} = 1.5\,\text{s/pax} \cdot \xi_{\text{alight}}$ with $\xi_{\text{alight}} \sim U(0.90, 1.10)$.

### 4.3 Zero-Demand Free-Flow Condition

To prevent artificial clogging on underutilized segments, if $N_{\text{on}} = 0$, $N_{\text{off}} = 0$, and the station is not a designated terminal turnaround, the bus bypasses the berth resource request entirely:

$$T_{\text{dwell}} = 0, \quad \text{Delay}_{\text{berth}} = 0$$

## 5. Rogue Actor Lingering Dynamics ("Nagpupuno")

In unregulated or semi-formal dispatch regimes, drivers occasionally linger at high-demand platforms to maximize fare revenue before departing:

$$\text{Condition: } \left(\frac{\text{Occupancy}}{\text{Capacity}} < 0.90\right) \land \left(|\mathcal{Q}_{i, \text{dir}}| > 0\right) \land (i < \text{Terminal})$$

When the condition holds, lingering occurs with probability:

$$P_{\text{rogue}} = p_{\text{base}} \cdot \mathbf{1}_{\text{hotspot}} \cdot 1.3$$

The additional obstruction dwell is drawn from:

$$T_{\text{rogue}} \sim U(60\,\text{s}, 180\,\text{s})$$

During $T_{\text{rogue}}$, the bus retains berth ownership, directly delaying downstream buses waiting in the physical queue and propagating localized headways:

$$h_{\text{actual}} = h_{\text{scheduled}} + T_{\text{rogue}} + \sum \text{Delay}_{\text{berth}}$$

---

## 6. Terminal Turnaround & Stacking Capacity

Terminals at Monumento and PITX are modeled as bounded staging facilities:

$$\text{Capacity}_{\text{stage}} = 15 \text{ vehicles}$$

Upon arrival at the terminal berth, all remaining passengers alight ($N_{\text{off}} = \text{Occupancy}$). Buses then enter the terminal staging pool for mandatory layover and route turnaround:

$$T_{\text{turnaround}} = 300\,\text{s} \quad (5.0\,\text{minutes})$$

Once turnaround completes, the vehicle swaps directional orientation ($\text{forward} \leftrightarrow \text{reverse}$) and enters the terminal dispatch queue.

## 7. Telemetry Extraction & Data Structure

The simulation loop captures system-wide state snapshots at uniform sampling intervals $\Delta \tau = 2.0\,\text{minutes}$:

$$\mathcal{S}_k = \left(t_k, \mathbf{P}_k, \mathbf{B}_k, \mathbf{Q}_k\right)$$

- $t_k$: Simulation clock timestamp.
- $\mathbf{P}_k = \sum_{i=1}^{24} |\mathcal{Q}_i(t_k)|$: Total waiting passenger count corridor-wide.
- $\mathbf{B}_k$: Vector of active bus states (spatial coordinates, instantaneous velocity, passenger load, active delay).
- $\mathbf{Q}_k$: Per-station multi-berth queue metrics:
  $$\mathbf{q}_{i}(t_k) = \left(|\mathcal{Q}_{i, \text{SB}}|, |\mathcal{Q}_{i, \text{NB}}|, N_{\text{bus, queued}}, \bar{d}_{\text{delay}}, \mathbf{1}_{\text{rogue}}\right)$$

### 7.1 Export Data Schema

The front-end client exports this dataset as a structured JSON payload for external econometric and operations research tools:
- `simulation_configuration`: Global parameters ($h_{\text{dispatch}}$, fleet size $N_{\text{fleet}}$, vehicle capacity, $p_{\text{rogue}}$, observation bounds).
- `corridor_telemetry_summary`: Global extremes ($\max \mathbf{P}_k$, peak bus queue, maximum delay timestamp, primary bottleneck node).
- `stations_summary`: Aggregated station performance indicators (peak queue, average platform wait, rogue occurrence frequency).
- `telemetry_snapshots`: Comprehensive sequential time-slices for external visualization and time-series modeling.
